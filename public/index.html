How Local Public Comment Works

Colorado city councils, county commissioner boards, and school boards share the same core public participation workflow:

  1. Check Agendas 48–72 Hours Early: Ordinances usually require two readings—weighing in before the second reading carries the most weight.
  2. Contact Your Representative Directly: Click any representative bubble in your workspace to email or call them directly, referencing the specific agenda item and your ward/district.
  3. Submit Written Comment Before Cutoff: Most Front Range boards close written comment 2–24 hours before the meeting so emails enter the official packet.
  4. Sign Up to Speak (2–3 Minutes): Use the Open Agenda ↗ link on any upcoming meeting to verify in-person or Zoom speaker sign-up rules. Lead with your name, neighborhood, and whether you support, oppose, or request a specific amendment.

Local Measures 101

Ordinances: Binding local laws (zoning codes, utility rates, municipal regulations) that normally require two public readings before taking effect.

Resolutions: Policy directives, contract awards, intergovernmental agreements, and budget transfers typically decided in one session.

Pivotal Votes: Roll-call votes decided by a 1-vote margin (e.g., 4–3 or 5–4) where any single majority vote flipping would have changed the outcome.

Impact Score (0–100): An open, nonpartisan formula weighting budget/fiscal scope, community reach, contested margin, and structural policy change.
